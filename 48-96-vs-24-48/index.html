Which one suits you

Both use three platoons and the same 56-hour average week, and both put 9 or 10 shifts into any 28-day 7(k) work period, so overtime and pay are about the same.

The 48/96 halves the trips to the station: about 61 commutes a year instead of about 122. That matters most for firefighters who live an hour or more away. You also get four days off in a row every six days.

The 24/48 keeps each stretch at 24 hours, so a bad night is followed by two days off rather than another 24 hours on duty. Swaps are easier too, because a colleague covers one shift, not two.

How to choose

Put both patterns side by side in the calendar with your own start date: weekends off, holidays worked and pay depend on where your crew sits in the cycle, not only on the pattern. Then compare yearly pay at your hourly rate in the shift pay calculator, which applies federal and state overtime.
