Hours a year = hours in one cycle × 365 ÷ days in the cycle. The exact number in a given year changes by a few shifts depending on where your crew starts.

Annual salary to hourly rate

Hourly rate = annual salary ÷ (hours a week × 52). Departments on a 56-hour week often divide by 2,912 hours (56 × 52). Your contract may use a different divisor, for example to leave out overtime built into the schedule.

Shift differential and overtime: a worked week

Night and evening differentials are part of the regular rate, so they raise your overtime pay (29 CFR 778.207(b) and 778.109). Take a week with three 12-hour nights and one 12-hour day, $25 an hour and a $3 night differential:

Straight-time pay48 h × $25 + 36 night h × $3$1,308
Regular rate$1,308 ÷ 48 h$27.25 an hour
Overtime premium8 h over 40 × half of $27.25$109
Total for the week$1,417

Working the premium out on the base rate alone ($12.50 × 8 = $100) would pay $9 too little that week. Enter your night differential above and the calculator does this for every week of the year.

How the overtime is worked out

Federal law pays time and a half after 40 hours in the workweek (29 CFR 778.105), on the regular rate, which includes night differentials (29 CFR 778.109). California also pays after 8 hours in a day and double after 12 (Labor Code 510); health care employers can adopt 12-hour alternative workweeks (IWC Wage Order 5). Alaska pays after 8 hours a day (AS 23.10.060) and Colorado after 12 hours in a workday or 12 hours in a row (COMPS Order #40). Fire and police departments often use a 7(k) work period instead: see the 7(k) calculator.

Questions

How much does a 12-hour Pitman schedule pay a year?

A Pitman schedule works about 2,190 hours a year, or 42 hours a week on average. At $25 an hour that is about $54,750 before overtime; with federal overtime on the weeks over 40 hours, a little more.

How many hours a year is a 48/96 schedule?

About 2,920 hours: 48 hours on and 96 off is an average of 56 hours a week.

Is overtime paid on a 24/48 schedule?

Firefighters at public departments are usually paid under the FLSA 7(k) exemption, with overtime after 212 hours in 28 days. Private employers pay overtime after 40 hours a week.
