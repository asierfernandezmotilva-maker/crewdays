Shift differential: what it is and how much it pays

A shift differential is extra pay for working evenings, nights or weekends: a fixed amount per hour, such as $2, or a percentage of your base rate, such as 10%. No federal law requires private employers to pay it, but once it is paid it counts in your regular rate, so it raises your overtime pay too. At $25 an hour, a $2 night differential on a rotation that is half nights adds about $2,300 a year.

How a shift differential works

Your employer decides which hours earn extra pay and how much. It is paid in one of two ways:

  • A flat amount per hour, such as $1.50 or $2. A $1.50 differential adds $12 to an 8-hour shift and $18 to a 12-hour night.
  • A percentage of your base rate, such as 10%. At $25 an hour that is $2.50 an hour, or $30 for a 12-hour night, and it grows with every raise.

For overtime, federal rules treat both the same. Night differentials go into the regular rate “whether they take the form of a percent of the base rate or an addition of so many cents per hour” (29 CFR 778.207(b)).

Which hours get the differential?

Each employer sets its own window and its own rule. Federal pay law alone uses three, which makes it a good map of the options:

  • Only the hours inside a window. General Schedule employees get 10% for regularly scheduled work between 6 p.m. and 6 a.m. (5 U.S.C. 5545(a)).
  • The whole shift, if most of it falls in a window. Federal Wage System (blue-collar) employees get 7.5% when most of the shift's hours fall between 3 p.m. and midnight, and 10% when most fall between 11 p.m. and 8 a.m. (5 U.S.C. 5343(f)). It is paid for the entire shift, at one rate (OPM).
  • The whole shift once 4 hours are at night. Nurses at the Department of Veterans Affairs get 10% for every hour worked when at least 4 hours fall between 6 p.m. and 6 a.m.; with fewer, only the hours in that window (38 U.S.C. 7453(b)).

Some employers pay weekends and holidays separately. A fixed night crew collects twice what a crew that rotates evenly between days and nights does: $4,576 against $2,288 at $2 an hour. Three 12-hour nights a week, a common hospital schedule, comes to 1,872 differential hours, $3,744 a year at $2.

The last column is the overtime effect. In weeks over 40 hours the differential raises the regular rate, so it adds to the overtime premium as well. That only happens when your long weeks are night weeks. On the southern swing in this example the 48-hour weeks are all day weeks, so the differential never reaches the overtime. Is a shift differential required by law?

Not for private employers. The Fair Labor Standards Act “does not require extra pay for night work”, and extra pay for night shifts is “a matter of agreement between the employer and the employee” (U.S. Department of Labor). It comes from your employer's pay policy or a union contract. Federal employees get theirs by statute, as above.

The overtime part is required. Once a differential is paid, it belongs in the regular rate of nonexempt employees for every week over 40 hours (29 CFR 778.207(b)). If your overtime rate is exactly 1.5 times your base rate in weeks with night hours, ask payroll how it was worked out. Whether paid time off includes the differential, and whether an employer can drop it, is covered under shift differential questions on the pay calculator page.

Taxes on shift differential

A differential is wages, taxed like the rest of your pay: income tax, Social Security and Medicare. The federal overtime deduction for 2025 through 2028 covers only “the pay that exceeds their regular rate of pay”, the half in time and a half, up to $12,500 a year or $25,000 filing jointly (IRS). The differential itself does not qualify. It does raise your regular rate, and with it the half that does.

Before you take a night or evening job

  • Is the differential a flat amount or a percentage of your base rate?
  • Does it cover the whole shift or only the hours inside a window, and what is the window?
  • Is it paid on overtime hours, training, on-call time and paid leave?
  • Is it written into a union contract, or only in a policy the employer can change?
  • Is there a separate weekend or holiday premium?

Questions

What does a shift differential mean?

Extra pay for each hour you work at less popular times, such as evenings, nights or weekends. It is either a flat amount per hour or a percentage of your base rate.

How much is a 10% shift differential?

10% of your base rate for each hour that qualifies: $2.50 an hour at $25, which is $30 on a 12-hour night shift. It also raises your overtime rate in weeks over 40 hours.

What does $1.50 shift differential mean?

$1.50 more for each hour that qualifies: $12 on an 8-hour shift and $18 on a 12-hour night. On a rotation with 91 night shifts a year that is $1,638 before any overtime effect.

Is it illegal to not pay shift differential?

Not for private employers under federal law. The FLSA does not require extra pay for night work, so a differential comes from your employer's policy or union contract. Federal employees are the exception: 5 U.S.C. 5545(a) and 5343(f) and 38 U.S.C. 7453(b) require it. When one is paid, leaving it out of the overtime rate underpays nonexempt employees (29 CFR 778.207(b)).

Does shift differential count toward overtime?

Yes. It is part of the regular rate, so overtime is paid at time and a half of base rate plus differential, averaged over the week's hours.

What hours get a shift differential?

Whatever your employer sets. Federal benchmarks: 6 p.m. to 6 a.m. for General Schedule employees, and shifts mostly between 3 p.m. and midnight (7.5%) or 11 p.m. and 8 a.m. (10%) for Federal Wage System employees.
