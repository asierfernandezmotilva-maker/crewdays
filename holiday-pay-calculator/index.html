Holiday pay calculator

Federal law does not make holiday pay time and a half. The Fair Labor Standards Act requires no extra pay for working on a holiday, and no pay for a holiday you take off. Time and a half or double time comes from your employer's policy or your union contract. When the holiday rate is at least 1.5 times your normal rate, your employer can count the extra toward the overtime you are owed that week.

Work out your holiday pay

Holiday hours: $450.00 (12 hours at $37.50). Other hours: $900.00. The holiday premium ($150.00) covers the federal overtime premium for the 8 hours over 40 ($100.00). Total before tax: $1,350.00.

Before tax, for one workweek under the federal 40-hour rule, with one base rate for all your hours. California also pays overtime after 8 hours in a day. Firefighters and police on a 7(k) work period have other thresholds: use the 7(k) overtime calculator. Nothing you type leaves this device.

Is holiday pay time and a half?

Only when your employer or union contract says so. The U.S. Department of Labor says the FLSA “does not require payment for time not worked, such as vacations or holidays”. Those benefits are a matter of agreement between you, or your union, and the employer. Extra pay for working on the holiday is also up to the employer. The federal overtime rules explain how to handle a holiday premium an employer chooses to pay, and note that the statute does not require one (29 CFR 778.219, paragraph (b)(1)).

The California Labor Commissioner gives the same answer for state law: nothing requires a special premium for holiday work beyond normal overtime after 8 hours in a day or 40 in a week. Other states publish their own rules, so check yours before you count on a premium.

Federal employees are the exception. A federal employee who works on a holiday gets basic pay plus a premium equal to basic pay, which is double time, for up to 8 hours that are not overtime. Required to work any part of a holiday, they are paid for at least 2 hours (5 U.S.C. 5546, (b) and (c)). On federal service contracts, holiday benefits come from the wage determination in the contract (U.S. Department of Labor).

Does holiday pay count toward overtime?

Pay for a holiday you take off

No. A paid holiday is not time worked. It stays out of your regular rate, and none of it can be credited against overtime (29 CFR 778.218). Federal overtime is owed for hours worked over 40, so 8 hours of holiday pay plus 36 hours worked is 44 hours paid but 36 worked, with no overtime due. Your employer can choose to count paid holidays toward overtime; the FLSA does not require it.

A premium for working the holiday

If your holiday rate is at least time and a half, the extra pay is treated as an overtime premium. It stays out of your regular rate, and your employer can credit it against the overtime you are owed that week (29 CFR 778.203). A premium below time and a half, such as 1.25×, goes into your regular rate instead and cannot be credited.

The regulation gives a worked example (29 CFR 778.219, paragraph (b)(1)). An employee on $12 an hour works 9 hours on the holiday at $18 and 41 other hours. The contract pays $654. A 50-hour week needs at least $660 under the FLSA, so the employer owes $6 more: the $54 holiday premium covers most of the $60 overtime premium. Enter those numbers in the calculator and it shows $660. If your contract pays the holiday premium and overtime separately, you get more than this minimum.

The same section describes a worker on 24-hour shifts who gets holiday pay whether or not they work the holiday, plus their normal pay when they do. That holiday pay also stays out of the regular rate (paragraph (a)(4)). Enter it in the calculator under “Holiday pay for time off”.

A holiday on a Saturday is a day off on the Friday before for federal employees with a Monday-to-Friday week (5 U.S.C. 6103(b)), and a Sunday holiday moves to the Monday after (Executive Order 11582; dates from OPM). Christmas 2027 is a Saturday, so federal employees have Friday, December 24 off, and New Year's Day 2028 is observed on Friday, December 31, 2027. If you are on shift that weekend, your policy decides whether the premium follows the actual date, the observed day or both.

Who works Thanksgiving, Christmas and New Year's on a 24/48?

A 24/48 has three platoons, each on duty one day in three, so every holiday falls to one of them. B shift works the day after A shift, and C shift the day after B. Find the platoon that has Thanksgiving Day 2026 on your department's calendar, then read down its column:

Christmas 2026 comes 29 days after Thanksgiving, which is 9 cycles of three plus 2 days. So Christmas goes to the platoon two places after the Thanksgiving platoon, and New Year's Day, 7 days later, moves one place on again. A 365-day year is 121 cycles plus 2 days, so a fixed-date holiday moves two places from one year to the next: the platoon that works Christmas 2026 has Christmas 2027 off. Across a February 29, 366 days is exactly 122 cycles, and the holiday stays with the same platoon, as Christmas does from 2027 to 2028.

Over a year each platoon works about a third of the 11 federal holidays, but the split is uneven. The platoon that works Thanksgiving 2026 works 3 of them in 2026, the next platoon 4 and the third 4. In 2027 the split is 3, 5 and 3. Kelly days and shift trades change who is actually on duty. And on a Pitman (2-2-3)?

On a Pitman each shift is covered by two crews that take turns: when one day crew is off, the other works. When the blocks are lined up so the 3-day block falls on Friday to Sunday, each crew gets every other weekend off. With that alignment:

What to check in your policy or contract

  • Which days count: the 11 federal holidays, fewer, or extra days such as Christmas Eve.
  • The actual date or the observed day when a holiday falls on a weekend.
  • The rate (1.5×, 2× or a flat amount) and whether you also get holiday pay for the day.
  • How a night shift that crosses midnight into or out of the holiday is paid.
  • Whether the holiday premium is credited against overtime or paid on top of it.

Questions

Is holiday pay time and a half?

Only if your employer's policy or your union contract says so. Federal law requires no extra pay for working on a holiday. Time and a half means 1.5 times your normal rate for each hour worked on the holiday.

What is holiday pay for $20 an hour?

At time and a half, $30 an hour: $240 for 8 hours or $360 for a 12-hour shift. At double time, $40 an hour: $320 or $480.

Is holiday pay in the US double pay?

Not by law for private employers. Federal employees get double: basic pay plus an equal premium for up to 8 hours of holiday work that is not overtime (5 U.S.C. 5546(b)). In the private sector, double time is paid only where the policy or contract says so.

Does holiday pay count toward overtime?

Pay for a holiday you take off does not: it is not time worked. A premium of at least 1.5× for working the holiday stays out of your regular rate, and your employer can credit it against that week's overtime (29 CFR 778.203).

How do I calculate my paycheck with holiday pay?

Multiply your holiday hours by your rate and the holiday multiplier. Add your other hours at your normal rate. If you worked more than 40 hours, check that the overtime premium is covered. Then add any holiday pay for days off.

What does 1.5 holiday pay mean?

Time and a half: each hour worked on the holiday pays 1.5 times your normal rate. At $25 an hour that is $37.50 an hour, or $450 for a 12-hour shift.
