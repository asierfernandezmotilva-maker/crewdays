Minimum wage by state (2026)

The federal minimum wage is $7.25 an hour, and it applies in 20 states that have no higher rate. The District of Columbia has the highest minimum, $18.40, followed by Washington ($17.13), Connecticut ($16.94) and California ($16.90), according to the US Department of Labor table updated on July 1, 2026.

The federal minimum wage

The Fair Labor Standards Act sets the federal minimum at $7.25 an hour (29 U.S.C. 206). Where a state or city sets a higher rate, the employer must pay the higher one. The federal law also requires overtime at one and a half times the regular rate after 40 hours in a workweek.

Minimum wage for shift workers

A shift differential is paid on top of your base rate and, under federal rules, it counts in the regular rate used to work out overtime (29 CFR 778.207(b)). A few states add daily overtime that matters on 12-hour shifts: California after 8 hours a day and double time after 12, Alaska after 8 and Colorado after 12. At $16.90 in California, a 12-hour shift pays 8 hours at the regular rate and 4 at time and a half. Questions

What is the federal minimum wage in 2026?

$7.25 an hour, set by the Fair Labor Standards Act. States and cities can set higher rates.

Which state has the highest minimum wage?

Washington, at $17.13 an hour, among the states. The District of Columbia is higher, at $18.40 (DOL table, July 1, 2026).

Which states use the $7.25 federal minimum wage?

Alabama, Georgia, Idaho, Indiana, Iowa, Kansas, Kentucky, Louisiana, Mississippi, New Hampshire, North Carolina, North Dakota, Oklahoma, Pennsylvania, South Carolina, Tennessee, Texas, Utah, Wisconsin, Wyoming.

How much is minimum wage a year?

At $7.25 and 40 hours a week, $15,080 a year. At California's $16.90, $35,152.

Do night shifts pay more than minimum wage?

Federal law does not require a night premium. If your employer pays one, it goes on top of the rate and counts in your overtime rate.
